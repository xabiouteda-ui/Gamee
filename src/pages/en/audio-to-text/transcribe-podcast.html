Transcribe a podcast episode to text, free and unlimited

Drop in the episode as MP3 or WAV, or the video of your video podcast, and download a timestamped transcript. Use it for show notes, YouTube chapters, a blog post or captions. Everything runs in your browser.

What a transcript does for your show

  • Gets you found in search. Search engines don't listen to audio. A page with the episode text, or a long write-up based on it, can rank for the topics you talk about.
  • Show notes and chapters in minutes. The timestamps tell you where each topic starts, ready for Spotify descriptions or YouTube chapters.
  • Accessibility. Deaf and hard-of-hearing listeners can follow along, and anyone can read it where they can't play sound.
  • Social posts. Your best lines, already written down, for quote cards and clips.

Step by step

  1. Use the final mixed file you publish, not the separate tracks, so timestamps match the released episode.
  2. Pick "Most accurate" on a computer. Long episodes take a while; you can keep working meanwhile.
  3. Check proper nouns: guest names, brands and jargon are what the AI gets wrong most. Click a timestamp to hear the line again.
  4. Download TXT for notes or the blog, and SRT if the episode also goes to YouTube.

Video podcasts

You can load the video file directly (MP4, MOV, WebM); only the audio is used. For word-by-word animated captions burned into vertical clips, use animated captions.

FAQ

Does it tell speakers apart?

Not yet: you get continuous text with timestamps. With two or three voices, adding names while you review is quick.

Is there a length limit?

We don't set one. The practical limit is your computer: hour-long episodes work, but they are slower on machines without WebGPU.

Does it work with podcasts in other languages?

Yes, choose the episode's language from the list, or tick "Translate to English" to get an English transcript.
